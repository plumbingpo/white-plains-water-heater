Hot Water at Home

Why Homeowners in White Plains Trust Us

A busy household runs through dozens of gallons of hot water every day, and a failing water heater disrupts the whole routine, from morning showers to evening laundry. Left alone, a heavy layer of sediment or a dripping relief valve can turn an inconvenience into a flooded utility room. When something goes wrong, you need a team that responds quickly, diagnoses the fault accurately, and repairs it properly. That's exactly what White Plains Water Heater delivers.

We stock the usual failure parts, including elements, thermostats, thermocouples, and relief valves, so many water heater repairs wrap up on the first visit, whether the heater is a gas tank, an electric tank, a tankless model, or a hybrid heat pump.

  • Tankless installation and descaling
  • Repairs and planned replacements
  • Electric elements, gas valves, and pilots
  • Leaking tanks, valves, and fittings
  • Tank flushing and relief valve tests
  • Emergency and same-day repairs
No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or installing a new heater. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection right away. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Annual Flushing

Maintenance Plans for White Plains Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service heads off failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At White Plains Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years gets a closer look than a new one, a tankless unit on hard water needs regular descaling to protect its heat exchanger working, and a large family cycles the heater far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before quoting a new heater, White Plains Water Heater looks at the details that determine your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, showers that run back to back, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and your incoming water temperature. That gives you a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your busiest hour without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Common Problems

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in White Plains

The majority of service calls we take come down to a handful of recurring problems. There's no hot water at all. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The hot water runs out fast, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where pressure is climbing, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
