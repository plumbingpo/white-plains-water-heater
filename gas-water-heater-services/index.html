Most gas water heaters in White Plains, NY work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across White Plains, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and White Plains Water Heater can take over from there.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
