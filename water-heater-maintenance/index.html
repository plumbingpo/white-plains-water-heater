If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across White Plains, NY, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
